🎯 Teach the Computer

Cats, dogs or hamsters? Tap to add examples. ↗ open
0:11 — 6 min. The heart of the hour. 1) Clear. Add ONE cat and ONE dog: the whole board splits in two — the computer's first "rule". 2) Press 🎲 Starter examples. Hover: dashed lines show the K nearest examples voting. Ask "is a big, not-fluffy animal a cat or a dog?" 3) Set K = 1, press 😈 Add a weird one: an island appears around one strange example — the model memorised a mistake (overfitting). 4) Slide K to 7: the island disappears. More neighbours = less fooled by one weirdo. Punchline: nobody wrote the coloured map. It came 100% from examples. Change the examples → change the AI. Invite 2–3 students to add points themselves.

Plot twist

✏️ Doodle Trainer

Draw → save with a label → draw a new one → does it guess? ↗ open
0:17 — 3½ min. Get 3 volunteers to draw 3 suns, 3 houses, 3 moons (or rename the classes — anything the room shouts). Then someone draws a NEW one: the bars move live while drawing. If it's wrong: save it with the right label — "that's literally how AI gets better: more, better-labelled data". Then ask someone to draw a sun really badly, or a star — it still picks one of the three. It can only choose between what it knows. Works offline (no model download).

👁️ How a Computer Sees

A picture is just a grid of numbers. ↗ open
0:21 — 4 min. Press 🎥 Camera (allow it; the sample picture is the fallback). 1) Pixel size slider all the way up: the numbers appear. "This is ALL the computer gets. No 'face', just numbers." 2) Slide back down to normal: 307 200 numbers per frame, 30 frames a second. 3) ✴️ Edges: wave your hand. The 3×3 grid of numbers is a FILTER sliding over the picture. Show blur / emboss. Punchline: a neural network's first layer learns filters like these on its own, then stacks them: edges → shapes → objects. (Next slide.) Keep it brisk: two filters are enough.

Deep learning, in one picture

Layers on layers of filters

📷 pixels〰️ edges🔷 shapes🐱 "cat!"
0:25 — 1 min. Each dot is a tiny calculator. Signals flow left to right. Early layers find edges, middle layers combine edges into shapes (eyes, wheels), the last layer says what it is. "Deep" just means many layers. Training = adjusting millions of numbers until the answers come out right. Next: YOU design one and train it in 10 seconds.

🧠 Build a Brain

Design the layers. Train on 10,000 digits. Draw one. ↗ open
0:26 — 7 min. THE deep learning moment: they design a neural network and train it live, in the browser. 1) Press 🏋️ Train with the default 64 → 32: ~10 seconds, ~95%. Watch the green line climb and the pink "mistakes" line fall. Connections turn cyan (yes!) and pink (no!) as it learns. 2) Volunteer draws a digit on the right: neurons light up, the answer glows. Try a sloppy 4, a 7 with a bar, a 1 with a hat. 3) Race! Press "no layers" → Train (~91%), then "big brain" (~96%, slower). The 🏆 table keeps score. More layers = more numbers to learn = smarter, but slower — and it can still be fooled. 4) Tap a digit in "😬 Digits it got wrong": most are genuinely ugly. Even humans would hesitate. Press 🐍 Python: the same brain in 14 lines of Keras. Offline? The digits are bundled on this site; only TensorFlow.js comes from the CDN.

📸 Train a Camera AI

Hold a button to record examples. Then test it. ↗ open
0:33 — 5 min. Loads an 8 MB model (MobileNet, served from this site) — give it a few seconds. Press 🎥 Start camera. 1) One volunteer in front of the camera. Hold "Thumbs up" while they show a thumb (~30 pics), "Open hand" (~30), "Just me" (~30). 2) Watch the bars jump live. Confetti at 40 pictures. 3) BIAS MOMENT: now swap in a different person, or stand far away, or cover the lights. It gets confused. Ask WHY → it only ever saw one person in one spot. 4) Fix it live: add examples from the new person. It improves. Data decides everything. Fallback: no camera → 📁 add photos per class. No internet → skip to the next slide.

From pixels to words

Can a computer read?

To a computer, a sentence is just numbers too — words become counts, and counts become patterns.

0:40 — 30 s. Transition. Same trick as the pictures: turn words into numbers, learn from labelled examples. Two quick demos: reading moods, then writing like ChatGPT.

😀 Mood Reader

Type anything. Is it happy or sad? Watch every word vote. ↗ open
0:40 — 4 min. Ask the room for sentences. Each word gets a score it learned from 179 sentences people labelled. 1) "my friends are awesome" → 😀. "school was so boring today" → 😞. Point at the green/red word chips. 2) The pink buttons are traps: "this movie was not good" → HAPPY?! It only counts words; "good" wins. Sarcasm ("oh great, another Monday") fools it completely. 3) Teach it: type a sentence, press 😞 Sad — the word scores change instantly. One example isn't enough to fix "not": that's why real AIs need millions. Link: ChatGPT-style models read word ORDER and context — next slide shows how they write.

💬 Mini ChatGPT

It read Alice in Wonderland. Now it predicts the next word — again and again. ↗ open
0:44 — 5 min. The big reveal: ChatGPT's core job is "guess the next word". 1) Click the top word a few times: the bars are real counts from the book ("after 'the White'… Rabbit"). 2) ✨ Auto-write with 👀👀 2 words: sounds almost like Alice. Switch to 👀 1 word: drifts into nonsense. Looking further back = more sense. ChatGPT looks back thousands of words. 3) Creativity slider: 🥶 = always the top word (repetitive), 🤪 = wild. ChatGPT has the same "temperature" setting! 4) ✍️ Your own text: paste song lyrics or a Wikipedia paragraph → it writes in that style. Bad data in, bad text out. Hallucination explained: it picks LIKELY words, not TRUE ones.

🩺 AI Doctor's Assistant

569 real biopsies. Which mistake is worse? ↗ open
0:49 — 5 min. Real data: breast-cancer biopsies from the University of Wisconsin. Each dot is a patient. 1) Press 🧠 Train: the yellow line swings into place as it learns. ~90% right on patients it never saw, from just 2 measurements. 2) Drag the ❓ new patient around: the risk meter follows. 3) THE question: look at 🚨 MISSED cancer vs ⚠️ false alarm. Which is worse? Slide the threshold down to 20%: fewer missed cancers, more false alarms. Doctors choose this trade-off — not the AI. 4) 🔬 Use all 30: ~94%. Better — but nobody can "see" 30 dimensions, so it's harder to explain. Ethics line to say out loud: the AI assists, a doctor decides.

…and what to watch out for

AI can be wrong — confidently

🤥

Hallucinations

Chatbots can invent facts, quotes and sources that sound 100% real. Always double-check.

🎭

Deepfakes

Fake videos and voices of real people. If it's shocking, check where it came from.

⚖️

Bias

Unfair data → unfair AI. You saw it with the camera.

🔒

Privacy

AI is trained on data — sometimes yours. Think before you share.

AI is a tool. It's only as good as its data — and the people using it.
